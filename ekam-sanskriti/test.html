Hinduism

Scriptures

Rigveda

ऋग्वेद

The Rigveda is the oldest of the four Vedas and one of the oldest extant texts in any Indo-European language. It is a collection of 1,028 hymns (suktas) organized into 10 mandalas. The hymns are primarily dedicated to various Vedic deities and describe cosmic order, creation, and ritual sacrifices. It provides invaluable insights into early Indo-Aryan society, their philosophical reflections, and their spiritual quest.

Samaveda

सामवेद

The Samaveda is the Veda of melodies and chants. It is closely connected to the Rigveda, as most of its verses are derived from it but set to musical meter. It served as a hymnal for priests participating in the Soma rituals. The Samaveda laid India classical music and dance foundation, emphasizing devotion and the aesthetic experience of the divine.

Yajurveda

यजुर्वेद

The Yajurveda is the Veda of prose mantras and ritual instructions. It is divided into two primary groups: the 'Black' (Krishna) Yajurveda, which mixes verses with prose explanations, and the 'White' (Shukla) Yajurveda, which separates them. It served as a practical guidebook for priests performing elaborate Vedic sacrifices (Yajnas), detailing the formulas and actions required.

Atharvaveda

अथर्ववेद

The Atharvaveda is known as the Veda of magical formulas and everyday life. Unlike the other three Vedas which focus on public rituals, the Atharvaveda addresses the practical concerns of ordinary people, including healing, marriage, and protection from harm. It provides a unique window into the daily life, beliefs, and early medical practices of ancient India.

Bhagavad Gita

भगवद्गीता

The Bhagavad Gita is a 700-verse Hindu scripture that is part of the epic Mahabharata. It takes the form of a dialogue between Prince Arjuna and his guide and charioteer, Krishna, on the battlefield of Kurukshetra. Facing a moral dilemma, Arjuna seeks Krishna's counsel. The Gita provides profound philosophical insights on duty, righteousness, and the paths to spiritual liberation.

Ramayana

रामायण

The Ramayana is an ancient Indian epic traditionally ascribed to the sage Valmiki. It narrates the life of Rama, a legendary prince of Ayodhya, his exile to the forest, the abduction of his wife Sita by the demon king Ravana, and the subsequent war to rescue her. It is a profound exploration of human values, duties, relationships, and the triumph of good over evil.

Mahabharata

महाभारत

The Mahabharata is the longest epic poem known and has been described as the 'longest poem ever written'. It revolves around the Kurukshetra War and the fates of the Kaurava and the Pandava princes. Beyond the central narrative, it contains vast philosophical and devotional material, acting as an encyclopedia of ancient Indian history, culture, and ethics.

Upanishads

उपनिषद्

The Upanishads are late Vedic Sanskrit texts that supply the basis of later Hindu philosophy. They are the most recent part of the Vedas, often referred to as Vedanta. The texts explore profound metaphysical questions about the nature of reality (Brahman) and the individual soul (Atman), shifting the focus from external rituals to internal spiritual realization.
